The top of an App Store product page used to be your screenshots. Since October 5 it can be a header: a wide image or a looping video that plays above everything else on the page. The loop at the top of this post is a crop of the one we made for [Automate It](https://automate.it.com).

Apple first showed headers at WWDC26 in [Enhance your presence on the App Store](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/205/), and they went live with [Showcase your apps with new assets on the App Store](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ljpl7kyn) on October 5, 2026. The same release added a separate image or video for search results, an Asset Library in App Store Connect, and a preview tool that shows how a page will look before you submit it. Headers show on iOS 27 and iPadOS 27.

## The Specs 📏

A header video is one file for every device. These are from Apple's [creative asset specifications](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/creative-assets-specifications):

| | Header video | Search results video |
|---|---|---|
| Size | 3840×1646 (21:9) | 1920×1280 to 3840×2560 (3:2) |
| Length | 5–30 s | 5–30 s |
| Frame rate | 30 or 60 fps | 30 or 60 fps |
| Files | .mov, .m4v, .mp4 | .mov, .m4v, .mp4 |
| Sound | Muted by default; people can unmute | Always muted |

Both loop forever. A still header is 3840×1646 too (or 5244×2950 at 16:9), with no transparency.

The classic app preview is still there and still follows its own rules: up to three per device size, 886×1920 portrait on iPhone, 15–30 seconds, 30 fps at most, made from screen captures of the app. A header doesn't replace previews or screenshots; it sits above them.

## The Rules 🚦

Headers get their own App Review guideline, [2.3.14](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), added on October 16. It says creative assets "must pertain to your app's content and functionality or overarching brand" and "should not include inappropriate content, references to other apps with an intent to mislead, unverifiable product claims, or specific or discounted pricing information."

Unlike a preview, a header doesn't have to be a screen recording. Apple's [asset best practices](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/asset-best-practices/) fill in the rest, and these are the ones that shaped ours:

- "Don't include specific pricing, discounts, website URLs, copyright symbols." Our ads end on "Try it free for 7 days" and our web address, so the header has neither.
- "Don't include logos or references to other platforms or marketplaces."
- "Your creative must work silently first." Ours has no sound at all.
- "Ensure your video is polished and loops smoothly without visible jumps or breaks."
- "Avoid quick cuts and fast-moving visuals."
- "If using text, use a short phrase that enhances your visual rather than describes it."
- Assets "must meet a 4+ age rating, even if your app's rating is higher."

## The Safe Area Is Small 🎯

The best practices say to keep key art "within the safe area defined in each template", but no page gives the numbers. So we downloaded Apple's [Photoshop header template](https://developer.apple.com/go/?id=photoshop-product-page-header-template) and read its layers with [psd-tools](https://github.com/psd-tools/psd-tools). The layer named "Art Safe Area" is 1646×661, centered, from x 1097 to 2743 and y 493 to 1154.

That's 43% of the width and 40% of the height. Anything that has to read, like a line of text or an app screen, goes inside that box. The rest of the 3840×1646 frame can be cropped away on narrower screens, so it should only ever be background.

## How We Built Ours 🎬

We kept to one idea: **AI drafts. You approve.** The line and a phone sit side by side in the safe area. The phone plays a real recording of the app's review queue, where you swipe right to approve a draft and left to ask for a redo. Outside the safe area, four dimmed columns of real draft cards from the app drift slowly up and down. On a wide screen they fill the frame, and on a narrow one they're the first thing cropped.

Everything comes from the running app, recorded on the iOS Simulator with `xcrun simctl io recordVideo` and driven by [AXe](https://github.com/cameroncooke/AXe). Two things made our first swipes look choppy:

- **Too few touch points.** A swipe built from about five steps makes the card jump every 90 ms. Sending it with `--delta 4` makes every frame of the drag a new position.
- **A development build.** A dev bundle froze for about 200 ms after each swipe before the card flew off. Serving the bundle with `npx expo start --no-dev --minify` fixed it.

The loop is 10.5 seconds and has to end on the frame it starts with. We handle the phone and the cards differently:

- **The phone.** We take 0.8 seconds more footage than the loop needs. Those extra 0.8 seconds crossfade into the opening 0.8 seconds, so the file starts where its last frame leaves off:

  ```text
  body = take[0.8 : 10.5]
  seam = xfade(take[10.5 : 11.3] → take[0 : 0.8])
  loop = body + seam
  ```

- **The card columns.** Each column is a strip of cards drawn several times, scrolled by an ffmpeg `crop` whose y position is `mod(t * speed, period)`. The speed is the strip's height divided by 10.5, so every column moves exactly one strip per loop. Our first version had four cards per column and drifted at about 220 pixels a second, which read as busy next to "avoid fast-moving visuals". Two cards per column halves that.

The line, the phone frame and the background gradient are HTML pages screenshotted in headless Chrome with transparent backgrounds. ffmpeg stacks them over the footage and exports 3840×1646 H.264 at 30 fps. The whole header is one Python script.

## Copy It With Your Agent 🤖

We had Claude Code build ours, and yours can too. Give your coding agent this post and something like:

> Make an App Store header video for our app from this post: 3840×1646, a 10.5-second seamless loop, our tagline and a phone recording inside Apple's 1646×661 safe area, and slow-moving app content outside it. No prices, URLs or other platforms' logos.

Before you submit, check it in App Store Connect's preview tool. Approved creative assets can wait in the Asset Library until you want them, so you can make a seasonal version early and switch to it when the season starts.
